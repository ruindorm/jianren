# Chapter Five — The Sword Gate Opens

**Long Aotian**: All this dithering and flinching. Is there no one here who can fight?

A striking man stepped out from behind the envoys, who parted to make way.

**Male Lead**: Wow. Waits for his underlings to finish talking before walking out. What a poser.

**Sword**: Master, you have encountered a rival poser.

**Male Lead**: (glaring at Sword) ……

**Long Aotian**: Open the Sword Gate and you'll know. No one dares? Fine, then. (He gestures; a subordinate hands him a plain sword.)

**Long Aotian**: Let's practise some calligraphy… (idly beginning to carve characters into the front hall floor)

**Senior Brother**: Stop! This is beyond insult! You — will you fight me yourself?

**Long Aotian**: I will. Receiving my Sword Gate challenge is an honour.

**Senior Brother**: Spare me the condescension! By this sect's Sword Gate — I, Qin Feiyu; sword, Skyplume — OPEN THE SWORD GATE!

**Junior Sister**: Senior Brother!

The ground shook. Wind rose. A strange dimensional space tore open across the sky, and every mind and body present was forced into slow motion.

A repulsive force welled up around Senior Brother, and two pillars of light pierced the horizon. Within one stood the Skyplume Sword, driven straight into the earth.

Senior Brother's eyes glowed red — his consciousness was already locked inside the Skyplume Sword.

**Gate Envoy**: Come, then! (Two men carry forward a sword casket.) I, Long Aotian; sword, Proudheaven — ENTER THE SWORD GATE!

The casket shattered. The Proudheaven Sword was drawn into the second pillar and stood upright in the ground, and Long Aotian's consciousness was locked within it.

**Everyone**: (holding their breath)

The pillars seemed to scan and read the two swords. Light churned through the dimensional space — and then, before each man, a sword was cast out.

Before Long Aotian lay the mirror of the Skyplume Sword. He pulled it free without hesitation and levelled it at Qin Feiyu.

Before Qin Feiyu lay the mirror of the Proudheaven Sword. A flick of his sword-finger turned it toward Long Aotian.

The true duel of the Sword Gate was about to begin.

# Chapter Twelve — Phase Two: Heaven's Lightning

A terrible sound came from above the inner space; everyone hurried to shut their eyes.

White light, and a rolling roar — and between the space of consciousness and the pillars of light, the world sheared apart in crossing fractures.

The dragon-form slipped through the seam and drove into Long Aotian's body. The **Dragon Convergence Palm** launched at Wu Ming's chest—

Powered by borrowed strength, the palm carried appalling force. Wu Ming sank his weight into his stance, calculating the timing gap between his sword's resistance and his own—

Both men had their eyes closed. The flash and thunder of the heavenly lightning caught Wu Ming between the assault without and the assault within, and the strain was brutal…

> **[The Outer Space]**

Between the two pillars, the final exchange was about to land—

Wu Ming (Sword Edition)'s fractal array connected the heavenly lightning directly to the Proudheaven pillar, forming a precision strike that could not be defended.

The Heavenly Dragon Morphing Slash struck the pillar holding Wu Ming's sword, and the blade was driven inch by inch toward the open air—

And then Wu Ming's body, on pure instinct, severed the tail of the slash…

Everyone held their breath…

A single clear ring — and the Proudheaven Sword was blasted out of its pillar first!

**Wu Ming (Sword Edition)**: **YOU LOSE!**

Long Aotian's palm was halfway through its arc… and his consciousness began to dissolve. Wu Ming braced to take the full weight of that strike — but his own consciousness was fading too…

The lightning ceased. The pillars entered the settlement phase—

**Senior Brother and Junior Sister**: WE WON!

But…

**Gate Envoy**: NOW!

Two envoys flung the sword casket open. The Proudheaven Sword was drawn in, and both men's consciousness snapped back at once.

The dimensional space vanished in an instant.

**Master**: Mm. So it does still exist — a forced termination of the Sword Gate… I never expected that casket to remain in the world…

**Senior Brother**: Your Transcendent Realm Sect is beneath contempt!

**Junior Sister**: I've never seen anyone so classless!

Then he turned and left without looking back.

**The Dragonrealm envoys**: Consider yourselves lucky today! We're leaving!

**Wu Ming**: (sitting on the ground, holding his head) Disconnected… ow… my head…

# Translation Notes

*Bastard Blade* is a **transcreation** of the Chinese original 《劍人》, not a literal translation. Comedy does not survive word-for-word rendering. This page records what was changed and why — partly for readers, partly so future translators into other languages know which jokes are load-bearing.

## The Title

The Chinese title 劍人 (*jiàn rén*, "sword person") is a homophone of 賤人 (*jiàn rén*, "bastard / scumbag"). The entire title is a pun, and it is untranslatable.

**Bastard Blade** reconstructs the same double meaning through a different route: the blade *is* a bastard, and it is used to cut down bastards. The tagline — *Slay the bastards. My sword is for picking up girls.* — carries the original's second half intact.

## Jokes That Had to Be Rebuilt

### "That's right" → "no cap"

In the original, Male Lead says the English phrase *that's right*, transliterated into Chinese characters as 辣死賴. Master, who speaks no English, is baffled and delivers a solemn lesson about not clinging to names. Junior Sister then "explains" it wrongly but confidently.

In English, Master obviously understands *that's right*. The joke is dead on arrival. It was rebuilt around **"no cap"** — modern slang an elderly cultivator plausibly would not know. The structure survives exactly: Master doesn't understand it, refuses to be bothered by it, and Junior Sister redeploys it enthusiastically.

### "you bitch" → "be… beach!"

In the original, Gate Boy mishears *you bitch* as the Chinese transliteration 優閉取, then repeats it as "be… 酷" (*be cool*). The humour is a child innocently parroting an obscenity he can't parse.

English keeps Gate Boy's question ("what does 'you bitch' mean?") and rebuilds the payoff as **"be… beach!"** — the same innocent mangling, the same beat.

## What Translated Cleanly

The technology jokes needed almost no adaptation, and are arguably stronger in English:

> configure a git environment first — so the results can be rolled back
> add that question to the search union
> according to my historical win-rate analysis
> executing contingency plan two — fallback path
> open book exam. Don't blame me when you still fail

These land identically in any language with software engineers in it.

## Names

Personal names are transliterated: **Qin Feiyu**, **Long Aotian**, **Yuan Ping**, **Yun'er**.

**Long Aotian** (龍傲天) is kept in pinyin for a further reason. In Chinese internet culture the
name is shorthand for an insufferably invincible protagonist — roughly the Gary Stu — and the
joke is that he is the *antagonist* here. Translating it would erase that.

**Wu Ming** (吾名) is the protagonist's name, and it is a problem. In Chinese it does not read as
a name at all: 吾名 literally means *my name*, and it is pronounced identically to 無名,
*nameless*. When he is forced to declare himself at the Sword Gate, what he shouts is, in effect,
"My name: My Name!" — which is why everyone present responds with question marks.

Transliterating alone would kill that scene: "My name: Wu Ming!" is a perfectly ordinary
declaration, and the confusion of the onlookers would make no sense. Translating alone would
break with every other name in the book.

So the book does both. He is **Wu Ming** throughout, and at the moment of declaration the line
plays in English with a one-line gloss of the pronunciation. Readers who follow translated
fiction will recognise the device; the effect is not identical to the Chinese, but it is not lost
either.

**Male Lead** and **Sword** are left as labels rather than names because that is what they are in
the original — the story breaks the fourth wall repeatedly (the sword mentions "the audience"),
and the placeholder labels are part of the joke.

## Register

The original is crude. The translation is crude. Softening the profanity would flatten the protagonist into someone he is not — his defining trait is that he is a foul-mouthed idiot with an even more foul-mouthed sword.

# The Sword Gate

> The rules as stated explicitly in the text, gathered here for reference.
> **Only what the text actually states appears here.** Inferences are left out.

## When a Sword Gate Opens

1. The ground shakes, wind rises, and a **dimensional space** tears open across the sky
2. Every mind and body present is forced into **slow motion**
3. Two **pillars of light** pierce the horizon, each sealing one true blade upright in the earth
4. Each duellist's **consciousness is locked inside his own sword**. Their bodies remain where they stand, eyes glowing red — Master calls this being "held in escrow upon the field"

## The Declaration

> I, ⟨name⟩; sword, ⟨sword name⟩ — **OPEN THE SWORD GATE!**
> (The challenger says "enter the Sword Gate.")

Male Lead's version is "My name: My Name! Sword: Sword! **Substitution — entering the Gate!**" — and will not activate until he makes a heart at his sword with both hands.

## The Core Twist

Once the pillars have scanned and read the two swords, each man is issued **a mirror of his opponent's blade**.

Which means: **you hold the enemy's sword, and use it to attack the enemy's own true blade.**

## Victory

As Master puts it: destroy the opponent's sword within its pillar, or drive it out of the pillar entirely.

A sword struck by sword-qi is forced out inch by inch (the Proudheaven Sword is lifted half an inch at one point). On substitution, it resets to its original depth.

## Outer Gate and Inner Gate

| | Outer Space (the Sword Gate) | Inner Space |
|---|---|---|
| Who fights | The two mirrored swords | The two consciousnesses |
| Visible to spectators | Yes | No |
| Effect | Decides the match directly | Determines **pillar barrier strength** |

Senior Brother's conclusion: **the sword alone isn't enough.**

The inner space allows negotiation — which is exactly how Long Aotian deceived Qin Feiyu with a truce, then struck.

## Phase Two: Heaven's Lightning

The pillars changing colour is the warning. As Senior Brother explains it: at the instant phase two begins, **the outer and inner Gates open a momentary seam**.

That seam can be exploited from outside: the dragons the Dragonrealm Gate carved into the front hall in advance converge at exactly that moment, and drive through the seam into Long Aotian's body (Dragon Convergence).

The lightning itself can also be redirected. Male Lead's fractal array wires it straight into the opponent's pillar, producing a strike that cannot be defended.

## The Price of Challenging

The rules as Junior Sister states them:

- **Escrow** — the swordsman and his sword are separated
- **Full disclosure** — every technique must be declared
- **Shame of defeat** — the loser must have their defeat carved upon their own Sword Gate

Which is why sects generally don't go around challenging each other's Sword Gates.

## Forced Termination

A **sword casket** can draw the true blade back before settlement completes, terminating the Sword Gate by force and returning both consciousnesses instantly.

Master's reaction on seeing it: "So it does still exist… I never expected that casket to remain in the world."
